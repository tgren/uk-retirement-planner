How the simulations work

The planner does not pick one future. After you enter a plan, you run a test. Both tests take guaranteed income first, then draw from your pots in the order you chose, apply simplified UK tax, and grow what is left — year by year, in today’s pounds — to see whether the money lasts to your horizon.

Replay historical markets

This is the usual starting point. It uses real US large-cap returns from 1900 through 2022 (you can start later on Inputs, for example 1950, to skip earlier crises). Years are not shuffled. The model walks every overlapping stretch of consecutive years that is as long as your plan. A 33-year plan is tested as 1900–1932, then 1901–1933, and so on. Each stretch is one retirement path, with your withdrawals and tax applied every year. From those paths you get a success rate, a median pot, and a best / worst range.

Monte Carlo (GBM)

Instead of history, this draws 1,000 random year-by-year paths from the expected return and volatility you type on Inputs. Those paths are not calendar years. Use this when you want a statistical test around your own return assumption, rather than what markets actually did.

Help & method has a How to use guide, plus a Method tab for tax, data and how the numbers are built.

    Guide

    Help & method

    What this tool is

    An educational planner for a UK-taxed household (England, Wales or Northern Ireland) with ISAs, SIPPs, taxable accounts and a US 401(k)/IRA. You enter a plan, then test whether the pots can fund your net withdrawal to a horizon age. It is not personalised or regulated financial advice. Figures are illustrative.

    Everything you see is in today’s pounds. A pot of £1m at age 85 means £1m of today’s spending power, not a future bank statement.

    The three pages

    • Start — welcome and a short description of the two tests.
    • Inputs — your ages, withdrawal, pots, pensions and withdrawal order.
    • Results — success rate, age snapshots, charts and the year-by-year table.

    1. Enter your plan

    Open Inputs. Tap the ? beside any field for a short explanation of that input.

    • Ages — current age, when withdrawals start, and the last age modelled (the horizon).
    • Net withdrawal — what you want left after UK income tax each year, in today’s pounds. The model may withdraw more from taxable pots so you still receive this net amount.
    • Success target % — used only when the planner searches for the companion “success-target withdrawal”. It does not change the success rate shown for the amount you typed.
    • Pots — add ISA, SIPP, GIA, cash or a US 401(k). On a SIPP, enter how much is still tax-free versus already taxable if you have already taken PCLS. Foreign pots and foreign pensions take any currency plus an FX rate (GBP per 1 unit). Leave a pot’s return blank to use the global expected return.
    • Income & pensions — State Pension and other guaranteed income, with a start age.
    • Withdrawal order — which pot is drawn first after guaranteed income. Use the up / down buttons or a preset.
    • Earliest historical year — 1900 is the full market sample. 1950 skips the world wars and the Depression. This only affects Replay historical markets.

    Changes save automatically in this browser. Use Save plan file for a private backup you can restore later.

    2. Run a test

    On Inputs or Results, use the bar at the bottom of the screen. Until you run a test, Results shows only a single expected-return line — not a success rate or min / max.

    • Replay historical markets — the usual starting point. Applies your plan to real US large-cap returns from 1900 through 2022 (or from the earliest year you set). Years stay in order; each overlapping stretch as long as your plan is one path.
    • Run 1,000 GBM paths — Monte Carlo. Draws random paths from the expected return and volatility you typed, not from historical calendar years.

    If you change inputs, the bar tells you the last run is out of date. Run it again before you trust the Results numbers. Market history 1900–2022 in the header charts the same return series the replay uses.

    3. Read Results

    • Success rate — share of tested paths that still have money at the horizon.
    • Median capital — the middle path’s pot at the horizon, in today’s pounds.
    • Portfolio depletes — first age the weaker (10th-percentile) path is empty. Worst case is the earliest age any path runs out.
    • Success-target withdrawal — the highest constant net withdrawal at which at least your chosen share of paths still succeed. It can be above or below the amount you entered.
    • Age cards — after a run, each age shows median, min and max pot at that age. The richest year at 70 need not be the same path as at 90.
    • Charts and table — portfolio over age, where income is drawn from, and a year-by-year tax and cash-flow table.

    Use Print / Save as PDF on Results for a readable copy. Switch to the Method tab for tax treatment, data sources and what is not modelled.

    Today’s money (real £)

    Everything on the charts and in the table is in today’s purchasing power. Your net withdrawal is the amount you want left after income tax each year, held constant in real terms.

    Expected return is entered as nominal. The engine converts it to a real return using your inflation rate: (1 + return) / (1 + inflation) − 1. State Pension is entered in today’s £ and treated as inflation-linked. Foreign pensions can be in any currency with an FX rate (GBP per 1 unit) and are also treated as inflation-linked. A DB pension only changes in real terms if its indexation differs from inflation.

    How a year is calculated

    In each retirement year the model takes guaranteed income first, then draws from invested pots in your chosen order until net of UK income tax covers the net withdrawal (or the pots run out). Growth is applied after that year’s withdrawals. Tax uses simplified England / Wales / NI 2026/27 bands (20% / 40% / 45%), Personal Allowance £12,570 with the £100,000 taper, and frozen thresholds in real terms.

    • ISA — withdrawals are tax-free.
    • GIA / cash — withdrawals are treated as taxable income. Optional taxable yield is extra deemed income (the pot is not reduced by that yield alone).
    • SIPP — you enter how much of today’s pot is still tax-free versus taxable. Withdrawals keep that mix (it grows with the pot) until the tax-free remainder is used, and are also capped by the £268,275 Lump Sum Allowance. Enter 0 tax-free if you have already taken the tax-free lump sum. If you leave the split blank, the model uses 25% / 75% (UFPLS-style).
    • US 401(k) / IRA — treated as fully UK-taxable. Required Minimum Distributions start at age 73 using an IRS Uniform Lifetime Table approximation. Surplus RMD is swept to cash / GIA.

    The 401(k) start age is not typed in. Discretionary draws follow the withdrawal order; RMDs still apply from 73 if anything remains.

    The two simulations

    Replay historical markets walks every overlapping stretch of real US large-cap total returns from your earliest historical year through 2022 that is as long as your plan (the full library is 1900–2022). That is the same sequence of consecutive years, not shuffled random returns. Min / max / percentiles and the success-target withdrawal all come from those windows. A later start (for example 1950) drops earlier crises and usually leaves fewer windows.

    GBM Monte Carlo draws 1,000 independent random paths from the expected return and volatility you typed (geometric Brownian motion; cash volatility capped at 2%). Those paths are not historical calendar years.

    The chart hint tells you which set you are looking at. Changing inputs marks the last simulation stale until you run it again.

    How to read the stats

    • Success rate — share of paths with a portfolio above £0 at the horizon age.
    • Median capital — 50th percentile pot at the horizon, real £.
    • Portfolio depletes (p10) — first age at which the 10th-percentile path is empty. Worst case is the first age any path in that run is empty (the earliest ruin).
    • Success-target withdrawal — highest constant real net withdrawal at which at least your chosen share of the same paths still have money at the horizon (default 90%). It can be above or below the amount you entered.
    • Min / max on age cards — lowest and highest pot among those paths at that age. Max is not one continuous “best career”: the richest window at 70 may differ from the richest at 90. The hint’s best start / worst start year is the single historical window with the most / least money at the horizon.
    • p10 / p50 / p90 — after a historical run, these are percentiles of those consecutive windows. After GBM, they are percentiles of the random paths.

    What the market data is based on

    Invested pots in historical mode use January-to-January real total returns on Robert Shiller’s S&P Composite (price plus that year’s real dividend), via the Open Knowledge datasets/s-and-p-500 series (Shiller, extended with FRED prices for recent months that lack a full real series). The embedded sample is 1900–2022 (123 years).

    Cash / GIA-cash in historical mode uses a real T-bill proxy: (1 + max(0, 10-year yield − 1 percentage point)) / (1 + CPI inflation) − 1. That is not a UK gilt or cash ISA series.

    This is a US large-cap proxy, not your exact ISA/SIPP mix or a UK equity index. Past sequences do not predict the future. The Market history button charts this same series. Earliest historical year on Inputs limits which windows are replayed; the chart still shows the full library.

    What is not modelled

    Scottish income tax, National Insurance, MPAA, inheritance tax, means-tested benefits, US withholding, foreign tax credits, sequence-of-returns within a year, fees, and rebalancing between pots. Investment pots are assumed uncorrelated in GBM mode. Tax bands stay at 2026/27 levels in real terms.

    How best, worst and median are chosen

    Replay historical markets does not pick the single best or worst year on this chart. It walks every overlapping stretch of consecutive years that is as long as your plan (for example 33 years from age 58 to 90). Each stretch is one window, starting in a different calendar year. Your pots, tax and pensions are applied to every window. If you set an earliest historical year on Inputs, only windows that start in that year or later are ranked below. The chart itself still shows the full 1900–2022 library.

    • Median — at each age the windows are lined up by pot size. The middle one is the median. Median capital at the horizon is that middle window’s pot at the last age.
    • Best / worst start year — the one window that finishes with the most / least money at the horizon. That is a whole career of returns, not the single best or worst calendar year in the tiles above.
    • Min / max on the age cards — the poorest and richest pot at that age across windows. The richest window at 70 need not be the same as the richest at 90.
    • Worst-case depletion — the first age at which any window has run out of money. That can be earlier than the 10th-percentile (p10) path.

    The “Best / worst equity year” tiles are only the highest and lowest one-year equity return on this chart. They are not the planner’s best and worst retirement outcomes.
